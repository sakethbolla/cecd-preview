Mission & Vision page
15 layout options

Each option is a full page built on the live site's header, footer, fonts, and colors. Same facts in every version; only the structure and emphasis change. Copy is placeholder-grade and can be edited in any option.

01 Statement first Closest to the current page. Dark hero carries the full mission; below it, Mission and Vision as two labeled blocks, then a short sponsors line. 02 One sentence The whole page is one fused statement in very large type on white, followed by three short pillars. Says the least, reads the most confident. 03 Split screen Mission on a black panel, Vision on a white panel, side by side at full height. A single line underneath states the founding facts. 04 Director's letter The mission is delivered as a short welcome from the Director, with the formal statements set small beside it. Human, credible, no project detail. 05 Four pillars One-line mission, then the work described as four numbered pillars (Research, Education, Transition, Convening) with two lines each. Structure without specifics. 06 At a glance Mission and vision up top, then a facts bar built from soft, verifiable facts (year, home department, founding partner, leadership). No dollar figures. 07 Guiding principles Mission and vision first, then five principles as a card set. Principles read as substantive without disclosing any project. 08 Editorial dossier Long-form prose with a sticky side navigation (Who we are, What we do, Who we work with, Where it is going). The mission is woven into the narrative rather than boxed. 09 Statement plus timeline Mission and vision at the top, then a short milestone timeline (1998 to today) with no dollar amounts. Shows continuity rather than grant history. 10 Image led Full-height photo hero with the mission over it, vision in a quiet band below, then a muted partner logo strip. Visual weight, few words. 11 How we work Short mission, then a three-step strip (sponsors bring problems, faculty bring expertise, results go back out). Explains the model without naming a project. 12 Statement and tiles A short mission, then six tiles that point to existing pages (Research Areas, People, History, Workforce, Partnerships, Publications). Depth lives elsewhere. 13 Poster Two full-width statement bands, red for the vision and black for the mission, in large type with almost nothing else. The boldest option visually, still plain in wording. 14 Then and now Two columns. The 1998 founding vision on the left, the mission as it stands today on the right. Makes continuity the point. 15 Charter A formal, document-like page on white with a serif pull statement and a Purpose / Scope / Approach list. Reads like an institutional record, not a pitch.
